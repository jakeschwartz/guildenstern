When we designed notifications (2026-11-10), the founder corrected me twice:
1. "'Jake handled it' doesn't get her to open the app. That's the bigger goal. Approach this as a product manager trying to drive enough daily opens that it becomes a habit."
2. "We need to try everything to test what works."

**Why:** I had been designing notifications around information and politeness (tell her when things are done, avoid noise). He cares about the behavior: does she open the app. A push that's complete on the lock screen fails that test even if it's pleasant. And with one real user, our opinions about copy are guesses; the data should decide.

**How to apply:**
- Judge any push or engagement feature by whether it gets the app opened, and whether opening it pays off. Payoff lives in the app (the rundown); the push should leave something to open for.
- Propose variants plus a way to measure them, not a single "right" design. State our guesses next to each variant and let the data overrule them.
- Still avoid streaks, guilt copy and "we miss you" nags. They spike opens briefly, then she turns notifications off, and with one user that's fatal. He hasn't objected to that line.
